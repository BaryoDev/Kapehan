Doodles · 18 of 24

Bigger drawings for the empty parts of a site

Same palette as the icons, different job. Icons label things. Doodles keep a page company while it loads, comes up empty, or breaks. Hatched by hand, cropped like photos, faceless so anyone can be the one holding the cup. Every one animates and every one can be stilled.

Placement

The sentence does the work. The drawing keeps it company.

Six rules for putting Kapehan in a product. Where a drawing goes, how big it gets, and when to leave it out.

01 · Icons label, doodles set mood

Two different jobs

An icon sits beside a word at 16–24px and repeats. A doodle appears once per screen, in a state the user did not ask for: loading, empty, error, done. Don't put a doodle in a menu row; don't scale an icon to 200px to fill a hero.

02 · Size ceilings

240 on a phone, 420 on desktop

Square states stop at 240px wide in a mobile column and 420px beside desktop copy. Wide scenes span one column of a two-column layout or bleed to a container edge. Past that the wobble reads as sloppiness instead of hand.

03 · Headline, one line, one action

Copy carries the message

Under every doodle: a headline that says what happened, one line that says what to do, one button. If the drawing has to explain the state on its own, the state needs better words, not a bigger drawing.

04 · No frames

Straight on the paper

Doodles sit directly on the page background. No border, no shadow, no rounded container. On a dark surface, give it a soft paper patch (the offline phone below) rather than inverting the ink.

05 · Motion is a whisper

One thing moves, slowly

Steam drifts, a drip falls, a cup bobs. One element, 3 to 6 seconds per loop, nothing faster than a breath. Loading is the only state allowed a continuous spin. Reduced-motion users get a still; so should print and email.

06 · Recolour the coffee only

One accent per brand

Pick a drink palette (next tab) and set its accent as --acc. The liquid takes your colour; the ink line, paper and hatching stay. That keeps the icons and doodles reading as one hand across every brand that uses them.

Palettes · 28 drinks and desserts

Every palette is something on the menu

Five roles each (paper, surface, ink, accent, pop), built only from the tokens the icons already use, so a UI in any of these sits beside the artwork without a seam. The badge says what each one is for: good for websites means paper is near-white and body text clears 7:1, so long pages stay readable; app · dark UI is a dark surface for product screens; brand · accents is saturated, best on a landing page or in small doses. How a palette is built

Paper is the drink's lightest layer at 4–6% chroma. Ink is its darkest, never pure black. Accent is the drink itself, the token the icon already uses. Pop is the garnish: foam, ube, pandan, a crema highlight. Use it once per screen. Surface is paper lifted one step.

Where it goes

Paste the block once into the stylesheet that loads on every page (globals.css, index.css, app.css). No component holds a hex; every button, chip and toast reads var(--accent). Change the block and the whole app changes drink.
